§1 — the fixture's red is the most important thing in the report, and it needs two more things

A missing re-point does not leave a residual, it blocks stage 3 outright — wallet_items.added_by → profiles is restrict. You called that right as the headline.

Two consequences. The restrict is load-bearing and nobody knows it. It is currently doing the job of a safety interlock: if the anonymisation is incomplete, the database refuses to finish the erasure. Someone will eventually look at a failing delete and "fix" it by relaxing that constraint to cascade or set-null, and they will be removing the guard while believing they are removing an obstacle. Assert every relevant FK's delete action in the catalog test, with a comment saying why, so relaxing one turns a gate red.

And the runbook needs a failure branch for step 3. Pause points cover "if you stop here". They do not cover "if it refuses". She will be in the dashboard, past the point of no return, having clicked Delete user, looking at an error she cannot interpret. Write that state out explicitly: what has happened, what the account looks like, that she must not retry blindly, and that the procedure stops there pending me. It is the one step where failure is both plausible and confusing, and it is on the far side of the irreversible line.

§2 — the parked decisions, ruled

entity_id — land C's statement now. Empty by use is a fact about today's callers, and callers change; open by shape is a fact about the schema, and schemas outlive intentions. Closing the shape costs one statement. Constraining entity_type is a schema change and can wait for daylight and a separate review.

home_currency — yes to the rename, and it is a precondition for the conversion work rather than a tidy-up alongside it. Two meanings in one column is not ambiguity; it is two columns sharing a name. Conditions: the migration changes no value; a test asserts every read site uses the new name; the money comment is corrected in the same change; and profiles.home_currency either gains its reader as the conversion target or is dropped — an unread column holding a plausible-looking value is a wrong answer waiting for someone to find it. Red lane, cleared on those conditions, PR left open for me.

Push toggle — remove. Leaving budgets.alerts_push in the schema is right: a column with no control is a note to a future implementer, while a control with no implementation is a lie to a user.

The emergency contact in the trip summary — I am overruling your (c). Removing it from the summary is the safest choice for disclosure and the worst choice for the thing an emergency contact is for: a printed itinerary that someone finds when the traveller cannot speak is precisely the scenario the field exists to serve. Keep it in, default on, and say so at the moment of export — one line naming what the file contains and a toggle beside it. That satisfies both standing rules at once: the app does not state what it knows to be false, and anything the user can add, they can remove. The notice describes it as included by default.

Verifying exports — neither option. Content verification moves to seeded test data, locally or on a test account, where the inputs are known and no real person appears. Her real account is used only to prove the mechanism works end to end on a device, and you read that file, not me. That dissolves the collision instead of trading one rule against the other, and it is better testing regardless: an assertion against data you chose beats an inspection of data you happened to get.

§4 — two consequences of the trip write path that are not about erasure

create-trip.tsx:258 is a direct client write to Supabase, not a queued mutation. So the one entity that reaches the server is the one that requires connectivity — in an offline-first travel app, exactly backwards. Establish what happens when Create is pressed with no network: does it fail loudly, fail silently, or leave a local trip the server has never heard of? Any of those is a finding; the third is the worst.

And fakeEngine.ts is honestly named but dishonestly positioned. Every child entity calls authorMutation into an outbox with no adapter behind it — a component whose contract is "I will send this" and whose behaviour is "I will never send this". That is the promise-in-another-system parent at architectural scale, and it is the largest instance we have. It is not a bug to fix this week, but it needs to be written down as a known shape rather than living in one file's name, because everything built on top of it inherits the assumption.
