§1 — you were right to test it, and the ruling is withdrawn

--cleanup=whitespace was a fix for a problem that does not exist. The line was never stripped; it is sitting in f6c712f at body line 15. Withdraw it, and keep row 68 as a correction in the struck-not-rewritten shape rather than deleting it — the wrong finding and how it was found are worth more than a tidy row.

Two lessons, and the second is mine.

A tool's rendering of an artefact is not the artefact. You read commitlint's echo of the commit and took it for the commit. That is the engagement's founding rule with a twist — the artefact was consulted, but through something that transformed it on the way. Add it as its own clause beside "verify the artefact, not the operation", because it is a distinct trap: you can do everything right and still be looking at a view.

And you named the right parent with the wrong medium. # as a comment marker is the interpretation parent — content placed in something that interprets it — but the interpreter was commitlint's display, not git's cleanup. Worth recording precisely, because a parent that absorbs an instance with the wrong mechanism attached starts teaching the wrong thing.

Mine. I took "git strips # lines under default cleanup" and ruled a change into the project's tooling on it, without asking how it had been established. It is a true general fact about git, which is exactly why it slipped through: a plausible mechanism is the hardest kind to catch, because it passes the sniff test that catches implausible ones. That is now twice this month I have ruled on an unestablished mechanism — the migrations gate's premise was the first — and both times the correction came from you actually running it.

So a standing discipline for me, which you should hold me to: when I rule a change into the tooling on the strength of a mechanism, I say how that mechanism was established. If my answer is "it is well known", that is a flag rather than a citation, and you should test it before building on it — exactly as you did here.
