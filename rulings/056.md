§1 — the column drop must assert its own precondition, the way your rename does

You did this correctly for the rename: the migration checks the count and the distinct set before and after, and raises rather than trusting the author. Then you dropped profiles.home_currency on the strength of "nothing read or wrote it, every row was the default" — a check made locally, against seed data, by the person writing the migration.

Production has eight live accounts and you cannot see them. The belief is almost certainly right, and "almost certainly" is not the standard for an irreversible statement applied by hand to a database with no backups and no point-in-time recovery.

Put the precondition inside the migration: raise unless every row holds the default, before the drop executes. Then the assertion travels with the change and is evaluated against whatever database it meets, rather than against the one you happened to test on. Same principle as the write helper asserting its own effect and the residual blocks counting before they act — a destructive operation checks its own precondition against the thing it is about to destroy.

That is the same family as the promise made in another system and it is now closed; closed instances are worth keeping, because a parent with only open instances reads like a complaint rather than a pattern.
