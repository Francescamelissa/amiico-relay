§1 — order correction, and the ambiguity is mine

The write helper is not the first task; the demonstrated red on the force-push guard is. core.hooksPath points at husky while our non-fast-forward refusal lives in .githooks/pre-push, and until a real non-fast-forward push to a scratch branch has been seen to be refused, we do not know that guard has ever been active. It is minutes of work, and you are now writing and pushing again — which is exactly the window in which an unprotected history rewrite would happen. Do it, then the write helper.

That said, you read it reasonably. I issued one priority order in one message and a different one in the next, and left you to reconcile them. That is my fault, and it is the subject of §2.

§2 — the queue becomes a file, and I stop issuing priorities in prose

Two of my messages have been lost and one arrived truncated, and now a priority has been scrambled across two that both arrived intact. Prose in a lossy channel is the wrong medium for an ordered queue: anything that must survive the relay lives in the repo. We applied that rule to rulings and to subagent instructions, and then kept the most order-sensitive artefact we have in chat messages.

So: an ordered queue file in the relay repo. I amend it; you work top-down from it; a chat message may propose a change of order but the file decides. Then a lost message cannot reorder the work, and neither of us has to reconstruct precedence from timestamps. Same reasoning as moving ordinal allocation to you — one authority, visible to whoever has to act on it.

§3 — the shellcheck red is a third instance of one rule, and you took the right fix

Rewording the sentence rather than reaching for a suppression directive was the correct call and not the tempting one: # shellcheck disable=… would have closed the red and silently switched off a real check for the life of the file.

The finding itself generalises, and it is the third time we have met it. A sentinel inside the value space of the thing it represents is read as a value. An example of a record stored beside real records is read as a record. And now a comment about a tool, written in the tool's own directive syntax, is read as a directive. One parent rule: anything placed in a medium that interprets it will be interpreted — describing a thing in its own syntax is doing the thing. File it as the parent with those three as instances; it will catch the fourth, which is likely to be a fixture, a placeholder, or a piece of documentation that something parses.

§4 — the denominator moved, and so did the clock

Thirteen gates at build 18, sixteen now; 43 seconds then, 70 now, of which tests are 19.5. Neither is a problem and both should be attributable. Record which three gates were added and when, and start recording per-gate timing rather than only the total — a suite that grows without anyone being able to say where the time went is one that eventually gets skipped under deadline, and the skipping is always justified by not knowing which gate is the expensive one.
