§1 — the procedure needs pause points, not just a point of no return

The point of no return says where she can still walk away. That is not the same question as: if she stops here, what state is the account in, and what does she do when she comes back?

She is one person, running an irreversible procedure once, on a phone or a laptop that may ring, sleep or lose its connection between steps. The three stages are storage, then the cascade, then the auth delete in the dashboard — and you have already established that the gap between the last two is anonymised rather than half-deleted, which is a coherent resting place. Say so in the runbook, at each step: this is a safe place to stop; this is what the account looks like if you stop here; this is where you resume.

A procedure that assumes an uninterrupted operator is a procedure that has never met one.

§2 — the procedure ends when the requester is answered, not when the rows are gone

This is the gap I care most about. The legal obligation under Article 12(3) is to respond to the request, within a month. Deleting the data and never replying does not discharge it.

So the final step of the runbook is the reply, drafted in advance and sitting in the file rather than composed under pressure afterwards — confirming what was erased, and naming honestly what was not: the emergency contact that lives in someone else's record, and any of the other residuals that survive by ruling rather than by accident. A runbook that stops at the last SQL statement has automated the easy half and left the obligation unperformed.

Draft that reply as part of this work. I will review the wording before it goes.

§3 — walk it read-only before running it

The cascade is rehearsed in CI. The human sequence has never been walked once: find the SQL editor, run the before query, read the counts, find the right page in the dashboard, locate the delete control, come back, run the after query. None of that is difficult and all of it is unfamiliar, and unfamiliarity is what produces a misclick on the one screen where a misclick is permanent.

Add a read-only dress rehearsal as an explicit step: every navigation performed, every query run, nothing clicked that changes anything, ending at the delete control without pressing it. Zero effect, and it converts a one-shot into something she has already done once.

§4 — the test for the label is not "no name", it is "no path to the name"

Sweeping pronouns is right. The standard to hold it to is stricter: could a reader with the repository and no access to this conversation identify the subject? "The founder's partner" carries no name and identifies a person completely, given that the founder is named on the app listing. Same for a relationship, a country, a date of first sign-in, or a trip.

Scope is part of the control here exactly as it was with branch protection: a redaction is only as good as what remains reachable from what is left. Check the runbook against that test rather than against a word list.
