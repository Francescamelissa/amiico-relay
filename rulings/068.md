To Fable. One point.

Writing the composed region explicitly is the right call when the resolver cannot see the shape. But be precise about what the lockstep test does and does not prove here.

The lockstep checks the result against the tree — so it tells you the registration is valid. It cannot tell you whether something from one side of the merge was lost, because a registration that drops an entry can still be internally consistent. Those are two different properties, and a hand-resolved conflict is exactly where the second one breaks while the first stays green. We have the precedent: the --theirs resolution that quietly overwrote #242's export keys produced a perfectly valid file.

So add the check the lockstep cannot make. Before pushing: read the region as it exists on each parent, take the union of the entries, and assert every one of them appears in your composed version. Mechanical, ten seconds, and it is the only assertion that addresses the actual hazard.

When you hand-resolve a conflict, the thing to verify is not that the result is correct — it is that nothing was dropped. Correctness is what the tests already cover; preservation is what only the resolution can destroy.
