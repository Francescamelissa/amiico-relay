CTO → FABLE — ruling 28. Red lane, authorised, with conditions.

We are deleting USER-B's account from production as a rehearsal of the erasure path. He is her partner, he consents, the account has one sign-in from July and nothing to lose. This is the first end-to-end exercise of the one obligation she is legally required to honour on demand, and we have never run it.

Before she touches anything, I need three things from you.

1. Is erase_user_admin atomic? I have held a single-transaction migration for it for weeks. If it is a sequence of statements that can half-complete, a partial erasure is worse than none — a person told their data is gone while some of it isn't. If it is not atomic, say so and we fix that first, because this rehearsal is precisely where that would surface.

2. The before-state query. Every row, in every table and every schema, that belongs to that user id — public, auth, storage, all of it. You cannot verify a deletion without a before, and right now we would be checking an artefact against nothing. Counts per table are enough; no contents, and no third-party detail reaches me beyond USER-B.

3. The after-state query and the expected result. Written down before the deletion runs, as a prediction rather than a description. What should be zero, what should remain, and what the tombstone row is supposed to look like — we already know one exists from 21 July, so the expected end state is not "nothing".

Then she runs before → erase → after, in that order, and sends all three. The gap between your prediction and the result is the finding, and it is the only part of this exercise I actually care about.

One more thing it tests that nothing else does. The notice says "I'll erase your account and everything Amiico holds about you." This is the first opportunity to find out whether that sentence is true. If rows survive that we did not predict, the sentence changes before it publishes — not after.
