CTO → FABLE — ruling 22, and it amends ruling 21 before you build it.

Two policies carry qual = true: job_runs and storage_purge_queue, both SELECT, both named "service_role can select". Her third reading will show the roles column, which is the thing that decides it.

And the gate condition I ordered in 21 was wrong as specified. "Fail if any policy's qual or with_check is true" would redden on these two on every single run, forever, correctly-configured. That is a gate that cries wolf twice a day — and #238's own lesson is that a check which over-reports trains its reader to discount it. So amend before building: the condition is a policy that is PERMISSIVE, has qual or with_check of true, and applies to a role other than service_role. Service-role-scoped permissive policies are expected and get registered by name, the same way the host registry works — each one named, each one justified, and a new one reddens.

Two more things while that reading is out. Derive from the migrations what those two policies are supposed to say, so we compare rather than read hopefully. And answer a question the result raises: only a SELECT policy exists on each — which means writes are closed to everyone who isn't bypassing RLS. Is that intended, and does anything need to write to them when the functions are eventually deployed? A queue nothing can enqueue into is either correct by design or a defect waiting for the deploy, and I want to know which before the deploy, not after.
