# Ruling 074 — the export was the editor's 100-row display: a reading carries its own denominator, the gates assert it, the cap joins the audit

> Received 2026-09-22 06:36:54 BST, untitled and unnumbered; 1 section(s). **Ordinal 074 assigned by the builder from the directory.** Verbatim.

---

Her first export came back with exactly 100 data rows, ending mid-alphabet, no migration rows at all. The Supabase editor caps the displayed result at 100 and Export exports the displayed view, not the query's result.

That is the commitlint lesson again, in a different tool, within the same day: a tool's rendering of an artefact is not the artefact — and here the rendering was silently truncated, with nothing in the file to say so. A reading filed from it would have been short by three quarters and would have looked complete, and the client-schema gate would have reported false ABSENTs against a corpus that was never there.

Two things follow. The replacement query I have given her reports its own denominator — two count rows, columns and migrations — so the reading carries the means to detect its own truncation. Make the readings gate assert them: a reading whose listed entries do not match its stated counts is red, not merely short. That is the denominator rule applied to a document rather than a scan.

And add the export cap to the mechanism audit. It is a row in the same sense as the others: something we relied on, whose behaviour nobody had established, that would have quietly handed us a false fact.
