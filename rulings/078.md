# Ruling 078 — the rehearsal found exact_match = 0: identity resolution is a gate before everything; similar_looking beside it; never confirm the address on file

---

Her rehearsal produced a finding that stops the erasure: exact_match = 0. The address in the requester's own message matches no account. One account exists with a similar-looking address; the requester's stated address is not in auth.users at all.

So the runbook is missing its first step. It currently goes: read-only rehearsal, then before-counts, then anonymise. Before any of that comes identity resolution, and it is a gate, not a courtesy:

Resolve the requester's stated address to exactly one account. exact_match = 1 proceeds. 0 stops and the requester is asked. More than 1 stops and comes to me. And the resolution is on the exact address in the request — never on a similar-looking row, a display name, or the operator recognising someone. Add the similar_looking count beside it, because a near-miss is the dangerous case and a procedure that only reports the exact match will not show the operator that a trap is sitting next to it.

Two things to write in with it. Asking the requester for clarification pauses the Article 12(3) month — that is what makes stopping cheap, and the runbook should say so where the operator can see it, or they will feel pressure to guess. And never confirm to a requester which address is on file; ask them to state it. The runbook's reply template gains a variant for exactly this case.

Record the rehearsal itself in the ledger as what found it. The whole case for step 0 was that unfamiliarity produces misclicks. It turns out its real value was that walking a procedure slowly surfaces a defect in the procedure.
