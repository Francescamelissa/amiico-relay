§1 — whose voice, and what it must contain

It is from her, to <the subject — relationship redacted on filing, 049 §4>, about a travel app. A letter that reads like a data controller's template will be worse than useless — it will be strange. Plain sentences, her own register.

Content, in this order: that the subject's request of 14 September is being answered; that the subject's account and the data held against it have been deleted; that it cannot be undone; what remains and why; and that they can complain to the ICO if they are not satisfied. That last one costs nothing and is the difference between a courtesy and a discharged obligation.

§2 — the part that matters: what remains

The subject's name and mobile number stay in <another account holder's — redacted on filing, 049 §4> emergency contacts, because <that person> entered them for <their> own safety and they hang off <that> account rather than the subject's. Say that in those words, and say that <the account holder> will remove them if they ask. Add whichever other residuals the readings actually show — the sign-in record, an invited email address — and for each, what it is and how long it stays.

The reply must not say "everything Amiico held about you has been erased" unless the readings say so. If the notice's sentence cannot survive contact with this reply, then the notice's sentence is the thing that is wrong, and we will have found that out from the one place that counts.

§3 — a confirmation written before the verification is a prediction

Draft it now as a template with the residual lines held open. Fill and send it only after the after-state readings are in, against what they say rather than against what was predicted. The prediction exists so that the gap between it and the readings is visible; the reply exists to tell a person the truth. Those are different documents and only one of them gets sent.

§4 — where the record of the reply lives

Two of our rules collide here, so resolve it deliberately: the repository must not carry the subject's address, and the compliance record must show that a specific person was answered on a specific date.

Resolution: the evidence lives where the personal data already is — her sent mail, dated and addressed, is the record, and it needs no copy. The repository records only that the reply was sent, on which date, to the requester by label, and which residuals it disclosed. The fact goes in the repo; the identifying detail stays where it already lawfully sits.
