§A — the rehearsal is not a rehearsal. Re-plan accordingly.

An erasure request from the second account arrived by email on 14 September 2026 at 22:06, from the address that account signed in with. Under Article 12(3) the controller's answer is due by 14 October 2026. That is the first externally-imposed deadline this project has had, and it converts everything we were calling a rehearsal into the real thing.

Nothing about the procedure gets faster because of this. What changes is that "we'll get to it" is no longer available, and that a mistake is now unrecoverable against a real person rather than a test fixture. The runbook hold stands and now has a date on it.

§B — the before-state readings are the only record that will ever exist

The database is on the free plan: no scheduled backups, no point-in-time recovery. So the counts taken before the erasure are not evidence collected alongside a safety net — they are the safety net, and they are the whole of it. They must be captured and saved somewhere durable before step 1 executes, not typed into a console and screenshotted.

Two additions to the runbook that follow from that.

public.trips goes in the before-state, with its rows, not just its count. Two Thailand rows exist and neither belongs to the founder's account. One of them may belong to the account being erased. If so, this erasure is also the experiment that tells us which trip is whose — for free, and once only. Capture the trip ids and owner ids before, or the answer is destroyed by the thing that would have provided it.

The prediction includes trips explicitly, written before anything runs: before N, after N−1 if that account owns one, after N if it owns none — and which of those you expect, stated in advance. Then the reading either matches or it doesn't, and either way we learn something. A prediction written after the fact is a description.

§C — ruling: the runbook names its point of no return

The procedure has a step after which stopping is no longer possible, and right now a person reading it cannot tell which step that is. Mark it. Everything above the line is reversible and can be abandoned without consequence; everything below it is not. A destructive procedure that does not say where its last exit is has one anyway — it just doesn't tell the operator, who is the only person who could use it.
