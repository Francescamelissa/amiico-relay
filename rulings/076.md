§1 — pinning the absence is better than what I asked for, and it deserves a name

My condition assumed a persisted payload to migrate. There isn't one, and rather than simply reporting that, you pinned it — so the day activityStore becomes persisted, something goes red and says why. That is the right move and it generalises:

An argument that rests on an absence must pin the absence. Conclusions built on "nothing writes this", "no such module exists", "this is never persisted" are sound the day they are made and expire silently, because an absence ending makes no noise. Pinning it is what turns a finding into a standing fact.

Worth a sweep on that basis, and it is cheap: which of our current conclusions rest on an absence, and which of those absences are pinned? From memory there are several unpinned ones — activity_log.entity_id being empty by use, the dormant uploader, the schema columns from 073 §2, and audit rows 9, 11 and 22, each of which is a statement that something does not exist. Some are already registered by name; the rest are conclusions with no alarm under them. List them, pin the ones worth pinning, and say which ones are not worth it and why.
