§1 — "failure with effect" is the worse of the two, and it needs a structural fix, not a reordering

Finding both defects and showing both reds is the right work. But the second one deserves naming properly, because the helper built to cure success without effect introduced failure with effect, and those are not equally bad.

The entire value of an error is the guarantee that the world is unchanged. Once a thrown error can mean "changed anyway", every error handler in the system becomes wrong at once — a caller that aborts leaves a dirty tree nobody recorded, and a caller that retries may double-apply. That is a broader blast radius than the original bug, which at least left the disk honest.

So fix it structurally rather than by moving the throw. Write to a temporary file, read the temporary file back, assert against it, and only then rename into place. Rename is atomic, so a failure at any earlier point leaves the original byte-identical, and a completed rename means a verified write. The assertion then happens while the change is still invisible at the real path, which is what makes "if it says it failed, nothing happened" true by construction instead of by discipline.

§2 — once an empty replacement is legal, "the content changed" stops being a valid success test

Watch the interaction between the two fixes. Deletion is now allowed, which means a legitimate operation can produce a file that is shorter — and an anchor that failed to match produces a file that is unchanged. If success is inferred from "the bytes differ", then a deletion of text that was already absent, and a no-op replacement, and a missed anchor all look alike.

Assert on the match count, not on the delta: the anchor was found exactly N times, N being what the caller declared it expected. Then verify the resulting content. "Unchanged" is a legitimate outcome and an error signal wearing the same face — which is the sentinel-in-its-own-value-space parent, turning up inside the tool we built to escape it.

§3 — the sweep comes from the catalog, and it has two tiers

You describe 045 §1 as running "from the migrations". The standing ruling is that the column inventory comes from the catalog after supabase db reset — the migration parser splits on ; inside $$ … $$ and filters out the very columns a conclusion is drawn about. Read the migrations for intent and comments by all means; do not let them be the source of the list.

And separate the sweep into two kinds, or it will either miss the hard cases or drown in them.

About a third party by design: a column that exists to hold another person's identity or contact details — emergency_contacts name and phone, trip_invites.invited_email. These are enumerable, and each one gets an explicit statement in erase_user and a row in the before/after query.

May contain a third party by accident: free text that can hold anything a user types — activity_log.summary, a wallet item's notes, even a trip name. These cannot be enumerated and cannot be cleaned by a query. They are handled by a rule about never writing names into prose, plus an honest sentence in the notice, not by a statement in the erasure function.

Report them as two lists. Conflating them produces a document where the four things we can actually fix are buried among thirty we cannot, and a list that over-counts its alarms trains its reader to discount them — including the reader who has to write the notice.
